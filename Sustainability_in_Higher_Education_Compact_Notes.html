Course Snapshot

Five outcomes drive the whole paper. If you can do these five things from memory, you are ready:

LO1 Understand sustainability in higher education and explain it in plain language.
LO2 Describe the SDG goals and the five sustainability competencies.
LO3 Understand responsible research & innovation (RADAR) in sustainability.
LO4 Explore institutional approaches to the SDGs (disciplinary, structural, dimensions).
LO5 Understand the challenges of ESD and evaluate progress with indicators.

Three units, five topics each — and one mind map per unit waiting at the end of these notes. Revision order: key terms → rapid-fire cards → mind map redraw → self-test.

1
Introduction to Sustainable Development
Definitions · SDGs in HE · Functions · Strategies · Competencies
Key terms in one breath
Sustainable development — meeting present needs without compromising future generations (Brundtland, 1987).
SDGs / Agenda 2030 — 17 goals, 169 targets, adopted 2015 by all 193 UN member states; "leave no one behind".
Target 4.7 — all learners acquire ESD + global citizenship knowledge, skills, values.
Triple bottom line — people, planet, prosperity pursued together.
Intergenerational equity — fairness between today's and tomorrow's generations.
Education about / for / through — knowledge of SDGs / agency for SDGs / institution as lived curriculum.
Five competencies (Wiek) — systems thinking, anticipatory, normative, strategic, interpersonal.
Whole-institution approach — curriculum + campus + community + governance changed together.
1
Brundtland's two ideas: needs (priority to the world's poor) and limits (environmental constraints on growth).
2
Four probing questions: For who (equity)? How (three pillars together)? When (2030 goals, 2050 climate)? To what effect (measured change in what learners do)?
3
Milestones: Stockholm 1972 → Rio Agenda 21, ch.36 education 1992 → UN ESD Decade 2005–14 → Rio+20 2012 → Agenda 2030 + Paris 2015 → ESD for 2030 roadmap 2020.
4
HE's double duty: SDG 4 as a goal (access, skills, Target 4.7) and all 17 goals as means via graduates, research, campus, partnerships.
5
Five HE functions: teaching & curriculum, research, campus operations, community engagement, governance — each answers "what does sustainable look like here?"
6
Espoused vs enacted: Lee et al. (2013) found mission statements promise more sustainability than universities practise — audit both.
7
Lozano's three phases: initiation (champions) → incorporation (curricula, research, operations) → institutionalisation (policy, QA, culture). Change survives leadership only after phase 3.
8
Strategy craft: visible low-cost early wins + pairing top-down policy with bottom-up energy; declarations (Talloires 1990, SDG Accord) anchor commitment externally.
9
Competency cues: issue maps (systems), 2050 arguments (anticipatory), naming whose interests count (normative), barrier-aware plans (strategic), role rotation (interpersonal).
Exam tip Compare education about, for and through the SDGs with one example each from a teacher-education programme — a guaranteed high-scoring answer when tied to Target 4.7.

2
Perspectives of Developing Sustainability in HE
HESD approach · University in society · SDG engagement · Environments · Pedagogy
Key terms in one breath
HESD — Higher Education and Research for Sustainable Development (IAU portal: iau-hesd.net).
Apprising & supporting — informing and equipping the community with guidance and examples.
Solidarity & networking — cross-institution communities of practice; SDG 17 inside the sector.
Conversing & convening — conferences and forums where norms and commitments are negotiated.
Third mission — service to society, beside teaching and research.
Co-creation / living lab — designing learning and research jointly with students and communities; campus as test site.
Service learning — community service interwoven with structured reflection.
1
University's four roles: educator (competencies), knowledge engine (research), social actor (third mission, public conscience), economic & civic anchor (employment, procurement).
2
Potential and perils (Chankseliani & McCowan 2021): unmatched leverage for the SDGs, yet risks of inequality reproduction, ranking-chasing, disconnected research.
3
Cortese (2003): no institution has a greater lever for a sustainable future — universities educate the decision-makers and model behaviour for society.
4
Engagement cycle: Commit → Map (SDG footprint) → Integrate (teaching, research, operations) → Partner (govt, industry, NGOs) → Evaluate (indicators, reporting) — and the loop repeats.
5
IAU 2016 survey pattern: teaching and research lead; partnerships and evaluation lag; staff support is the missing middle.
6
THE Impact Rankings (2019–): first global table scoring universities on SDG performance, not prestige.
7
Enabling environment = five traits: physical & psychological safety, participation, resources and time, institutional support (workload, promotion), assessment aligned to action.
8
Pedagogical toolbox: POPBL (Yasin & Rahman 2011), case dilemmas, service learning, living labs, blended/flipped — method is not a guarantee; structure + debrief matter.
9
Assessment is the hidden curriculum: if exams reward recall, projects die — align assessment with action competence.
Exam tip Walk a fictional college through the five-step SDG engagement cycle, one concrete action per step — then name the step most often skipped (Evaluate) and its cost.

3
Barriers, Problems and Future Challenges
Three Horizons · RADAR · HEI approaches · Sustainable teaching · Indicators
Key terms in one breath
Three Horizons — H1 dominant-declining; H2 innovation zone; H3 sustainable vision (Curry & Hodgson).
Curriculum mainstreaming — sustainability across all programmes, not one course.
RADAR — Responsible, Attentive, Disruptive, Authentic, Regenerative research qualities.
Institutionalisation trap — structures preserve old innovations while blocking new ones (Trechsel et al., 2018).
Disciplinary / structural / dimensions — three institutional approaches to the SDGs.
Sustainable teaching — teaching that endures: realistic workload + learning that lasts.
ESD indicators — policy / practice / outcome signals of real progress (Veidemane, 2022).
Greenwashing — image without practice; the test every indicator must pass.
1
Curriculum barriers: overload, silos, untrained staff, exam-driven assessment (Fernandez-Sanchez et al., 2014) — each implies its counter-move.
2
Horizons logic: H1 is not the enemy but the incubator; change agents work the H1–H2 boundary to grow H3.
3
RADAR practice: anticipate with stakeholders (R), watch real impacts incl. marginal voices (A), question inherited assumptions (D), honest values-aligned methods (A), give back more than you take (R).
4
Approach trade-offs: disciplinary = depth but silos; structural = collaboration but hollow shells without culture; dimensions = audit lens that catches blind spots.
5
Teaching shifts: coverage→mastery; performer→designer; grades→purpose; private territory→collaborative practice; content volume→competency depth.
6
Indicator types: policy (existence, budget), practice (% SDG-mapped courses, energy data, community hours), outcomes (competency portfolios, graduate action).
7
Measurement challenges: self-report inflation, definitional variance, values that resist counting — read trends, ask who prepared the data, pair numbers with site visits.
8
Swiss lesson (Trechsel): formalising ESD protects it and freezes it — design structures that can keep changing.
Exam tip Place three real innovations (an elective, a green audit, a revised curriculum) on the H1–H2–H3 map and defend each placement — the framework question examiners love.

Syllabus fieldwork

Engagement Tasks Checklist

Six guided tasks link the course to your institution. Tick when the output is in your field notebook.

Task 1 — Campus sustainability snapshot: one paragraph per HE function with evidence (policy, interviews, photos).
Task 2 — HESD research areas: six areas listed, localised, with one research question per weak area.
Task 3 — Pedagogical innovations: inventory of local innovations classified by type and competencies engaged.
Task 4 — Curriculum critique: about/for/through mapping + RADAR lens, ending in three costed recommendations.
Task 5 — Classroom environment audit: five traits rated before/after one low-cost intervention.
Task 6 — ESD indicator set: policy + practice + outcome indicators, each passing the greenwashing test.
Final gate

Twelve-Question Self-Test

Rapid recall
1. Who authored the 1987 definition of sustainable development, and what are its two central ideas?
2. How many goals, targets and countries are behind Agenda 2030?
3. State SDG Target 4.7 in one sentence.
4. Name the three orientations of sustainability education and one example of each.
5. List the five key HE functions and one sustainability practice for each.
6. Name Wiek et al.’s five competencies and the classroom signal of any one.
7. What are Lozano’s three phases, and which phase makes change survive leadership change?
8. Expand the IAU’s four HESD actions and name three HESD research areas.
9. Give the university’s four societal roles and one “peril” named by Chankseliani & McCowan.
10. Draw the five-step SDG engagement cycle from memory.
11. Expand RADAR and match each quality to one research habit.
12. Distinguish policy, practice and outcome ESD indicators with one example each, and explain the institutionalisation trap.
Check: 1–WCED/Brundtland; needs + limits. 2–17 / 169 / 193. 3–ESD + global citizenship for all learners. 4–about (lecture on goals), for (change project), through (green campus as curriculum). 5–teaching, research, operations, engagement, governance. 6–systems, anticipatory, normative, strategic, interpersonal. 7–initiation, incorporation, institutionalisation; the third. 8–apprise & support, solidarity & network, converse & convene, research; e.g. curriculum, indicators, governance. 9–educator, knowledge engine, social actor, anchor; e.g. reproducing inequality. 10–Commit→Map→Integrate→Partner→Evaluate. 11–Responsible, Attentive, Disruptive, Authentic, Regenerative. 12–policy existence / % SDG courses / competency portfolios; structures freeze yesterday’s innovations (Trechsel).

Score yourself honestly: 10–12 — examination ready; 7–9 — revisit the mind map of the weakest unit; ≤6 — return to the full course pack for one focused evening, then retake. Pair every revision session with one engagement task: the ideas only become yours when they touch your own campus.
