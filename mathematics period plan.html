Art-Integrated Lesson Plan

Class: IX Mathematics | Chapter: Surface Areas and Volumes

Duration: 5 Periods | Pedagogy: Art-Integrated, Experiential Learning

PERIOD 1: Introduction to 2D and 3D Figures & Surface Area of Cuboid

Teaching Steps Teacher Activity Learner Activity Black Board Work TLM
I. INTRODUCTION (3-5 min)
*Mind Mapping* (2-3 min)
*Probing Questions* (2-3 min)
*Declaration of Topic* (2 min)		 Draws a web on the board connecting 'Shapes' to '2D' and '3D'. Asks: "What is the difference between a square and a cuboid?" "How many faces does a cuboid have?" Declares the topic: "Surface Area of a Cuboid". Participate in mind mapping, answering that 2D has length and breadth, while 3D has length, breadth, and height. Note down the topic. Topic: Surface Area of Cuboid
2D: Length, Breadth
3D: Length, Breadth, Height
Cuboid: 6 faces, 12 edges, 8 corners		 Blackboard, Chalk
II. READING THE TEXTBOOK (5 min)
*Identifying Keywords* (2-3 min)		 Asks students to open the textbook to page 214 and read paragraphs 10.1 and 10.2 silently. Instructs them to underline important terms. Read the text silently and underline keywords: Two-dimensional, Three-dimensional, Solid figures, Cuboid, Lateral Surface, Total Surface Area. Keywords:
- 2D & 3D figures
- Faces, Edges, Corners
- Lateral Surface Area
- Total Surface Area		 Textbook
III. GROUP DISCUSSION (5 min)
*Concept Understanding* (5-7 min)		 Divides class into groups. Guides them to visualize cutting open a cuboid to form a net. Facilitates the derivation of TSA and LSA formulas. Discuss in groups how a cuboid consists of 3 pairs of identical rectangles. Derive that TSA = 2(lb + bh + lh) and LSA = 2h(l + b). Formulas:
TSA = 2(lb + bh + lh)
LSA = 2h(l + b)
*(Draw net of cuboid)*		 Chart paper showing cuboid net
IV. ART INTEGRATED ACTIVITY (10 min) Distributes coloured chart papers. Guides students to draw and cut out 6 rectangles (3 pairs) and assemble them into a colourful cuboid net. Measure and cut 6 rectangles from different coloured sheets. Fold and paste them to form a 3D cuboid, identifying top, bottom, and lateral faces. *(Display area on BB for students to show their models briefly)* Coloured chart papers, Scissors, Glue, Ruler
V. CONCLUSION & SUMMARIZING (3 min) Summarizes that the total surface area is the sum of the areas of all 6 rectangular faces, while lateral area excludes the top and bottom. Listen, note down the summary, and cross-verify with the art model created. Summary:
TSA = Sum of 6 faces
LSA = Sum of 4 lateral faces		 -
VI. CULMINATORY ACTIVITIES - EVALUATION (2 min) Poses 4 oral questions to assess understanding. Answer the questions orally. Evaluation:
Q1: Find TSA of cuboid (l=5, b=3, h=2).
Ans: 2(15+6+10) = 62 cm²
Q2: Find LSA of cuboid (l=5, b=3, h=2).
Ans: 2×2(5+3) = 32 cm²
Q3: How many faces does a cuboid have?
Ans: 6
Q4: Give the formula for TSA.
Ans: 2(lb + bh + lh)		 -
Home Task (1 min) Assigns specific homework from the textbook. Note down the homework. Home Task:
1. Find the TSA and LSA of a cuboid with l=8m, b=5m, h=3m.
2. Draw a neat net of a cuboid in your notebook.		 -

PERIOD 2: Surface Area and Volume of Cube & Volume of Prism

Teaching Steps Teacher Activity Learner Activity Black Board Work TLM
I. INTRODUCTION (3-5 min)
*Mind Mapping* (2-3 min)
*Probing Questions* (2-3 min)
*Declaration of Topic* (2 min)		 Draws a mind map linking 'Cuboid' to 'Special Case: Cube'. Asks: "What happens when length, breadth, and height of a cuboid are equal?" Declares the topic. Respond that it becomes a cube. Understand that a cube has all edges equal. Note down the topic. Topic: Cube & Volume of Prism
Cuboid (l, b, h)
↓ (if l=b=h=a)
Cube (edge = a)		 Blackboard, Chalk
II. READING THE TEXTBOOK (5 min)
*Identifying Keywords* (2-3 min)		 Asks students to read page 217 (Try This, Do This). Asks them to identify keywords related to space occupied. Read the text and underline keywords: Edge, Volume, Capacity, Right Prism, Area of base. Keywords:
- Edge
- Volume
- Capacity
- Right Prism		 Textbook
III. GROUP DISCUSSION (5 min)
*Concept Understanding* (5-7 min)		 Guides groups to substitute l=b=h=a in cuboid formulas to get cube formulas. Explains volume as space occupied and derives Volume of Right Prism. Substitute 'a' in TSA and LSA formulas. Discuss that volume of a prism is Area of base × Height. Formulas:
TSA of Cube = 6a²
LSA of Cube = 4a²
Vol of Cube = a³
Vol of Prism = Area of base × h		 3D Shape models
IV. ART INTEGRATED ACTIVITY (10 min) Provides thick cardboard or clay and straws. Asks students to construct a 3D cube model, labelling the edges and faces. Use straws and clay to build a cube skeleton, or cut and fold cardboard to make a solid 3D cube model. Label edge 'a'. *(Students display their 3D cube models)* Straws, Clay, Cardboard, Markers
V. CONCLUSION & SUMMARIZING (3 min) Summarizes that a cube is a special cuboid and volume is measured in cubic units. Write down the summary and relate the 3D model to the formulas. Summary:
For Cube, l=b=h=a
Vol = Base Area × Height		 -
VI. CULMINATORY ACTIVITIES - EVALUATION (2 min) Asks oral evaluation questions. Answer the questions orally. Evaluation:
Q1: Find TSA of cube with edge 4 cm.
Ans: 6×4² = 96 cm²
Q2: Find volume of cube with edge 10 cm.
Ans: 10³ = 1000 cm³
Q3: What is the LSA of a cube?
Ans: 4a²
Q4: Formula for volume of a prism?
Ans: Area of base × height		 -
Home Task (1 min) Assigns homework. Note down the homework. Home Task:
1. The volume of a cube is 1728 cm³. Find its total surface area.
2. Find the volume of a cuboid with l=12cm, b=10cm, h=8cm.
3. Draw a right triangular prism.		 -

PERIOD 3: Cylinder - Curved Surface Area, Total Surface Area & Volume

Teaching Steps Teacher Activity Learner Activity Black Board Work TLM
I. INTRODUCTION (3-5 min)
*Mind Mapping* (2-3 min)
*Probing Questions* (2-3 min)
*Declaration of Topic* (2 min)		 Creates a mind map of 'Cylindrical objects' (pipes, drums). Asks: "What shape do you get when you rotate a rectangle?" Declares the topic. Brainstorm and list daily life cylindrical objects. Answer: Cylinder. Note down the topic. Topic: Right Circular Cylinder
Objects: Pipes, Tins, Wells
Rectangle → Rotation → Cylinder		 Blackboard, Chalk
II. READING THE TEXTBOOK (5 min)
*Identifying Keywords* (2-3 min)		 Directs students to read page 220-222. Asks them to circle the terms related to the parts of a cylinder. Read the text and identify keywords: Right circular cylinder, Radius, Height, Curved Surface Area, Total Surface Area. Keywords:
- Right Circular Cylinder
- Axis
- Circumference
- 2πr		 Textbook
III. GROUP DISCUSSION (5 min)
*Concept Understanding* (5-7 min)		 Facilitates discussion on unfolding a cylinder into a rectangle. Derives CSA = 2πrh, TSA = 2πr(r + h), and Volume = πr²h. Discuss how the length of the rectangle becomes the circumference (2πr) and breadth becomes height (h). Derive formulas. Formulas:
CSA = 2πrh
TSA = 2πr(r + h)
Vol = πr²h		 Chart showing cylinder net
IV. ART INTEGRATED ACTIVITY (10 min) Distributes rectangular chart paper strips. Guides students to roll them into cylinders and observe the relationship between the rectangle's dimensions and the cylinder's dimensions. Take a rectangle of known l and b. Roll it along length and then along breadth to form two different cylinders. Measure and observe r and h. *(Draw diagram showing rectangle rolling into cylinder)* Rectangular chart paper strips, Cello tape
V. CONCLUSION & SUMMARIZING (3 min) Concludes that the CSA of a cylinder is simply the area of its net (rectangle). Summarize the learning and correlate the art activity with the formula derivation. Summary:
CSA = Area of rectangular net
l = 2πr, b = h		 -
VI. CULMINATORY ACTIVITIES - EVALUATION (2 min) Poses 4 oral questions. Answer the questions orally. Evaluation:
Q1: Find CSA of cylinder (r=3.5cm, h=10cm). [π=22/7]
Ans: 2×(22/7)×3.5×10 = 220 cm²
Q2: Find TSA if r=7cm, h=10cm.
Ans: 2×(22/7)×7(7+10) = 748 cm²
Q3: What is the formula for volume?
Ans: πr²h
Q4: What shape do we get when we unfold a cylinder?
Ans: Rectangle		 -
Home Task (1 min) Assigns homework. Note down the homework. Home Task:
1. A closed cylindrical tank has radius 7m and height 3m. Find its TSA. (Take π=22/7)
2. Find the volume of a cylinder whose radius is 10.5cm and height is 14cm.		 -

PERIOD 4: Cone - Curved Surface Area, Total Surface Area & Volume

Teaching Steps Teacher Activity Learner Activity Black Board Work TLM
I. INTRODUCTION (3-5 min)
*Mind Mapping* (2-3 min)
*Probing Questions* (2-3 min)
*Declaration of Topic* (2 min)		 Maps 'Conical shapes' (joker caps, tents). Asks: "Is the height of a cone the same as its slant height?" Declares the topic. Recall objects shaped like cones. Answer: No, height is perpendicular, slant height is along the surface. Topic: Right Circular Cone
Objects: Caps, Tents, Funnels
Height (h) ≠ Slant Height (l)		 Blackboard, Chalk
II. READING THE TEXTBOOK (5 min)
*Identifying Keywords* (2-3 min)		 Asks students to read page 226-230. Instructs them to highlight terms specific to cones. Read the text and underline keywords: Vertex, Slant height, Sector, Arc length. Keywords:
- Right Circular Cone
- Vertex
- Slant height (l)
- Sector		 Textbook
III. GROUP DISCUSSION (5 min)
*Concept Understanding* (5-7 min)		 Guides groups to derive l² = h² + r² using Pythagoras theorem. Explains the sector-to-cone activity to derive CSA = πrl and Vol = 1/3 πr²h. Apply Pythagoras theorem in the cross-section of the cone. Discuss how 3 cones fill 1 cylinder of the same base and height. Formulas:
l² = h² + r²
CSA = πrl
TSA = πr(l + r)
Vol = (1/3)πr²h		 Chart of cone cross-section
IV. ART INTEGRATED ACTIVITY (10 min) Gives students circular paper sheets. Guides them to cut a sector and fold it into a cone to visually prove the CSA concept. Draw a circle, cut out a sector (e.g., 120° or 90°), bring the radial edges together, and paste to form a cone. Observe the arc becomes the base. *(Show diagram of sector AOB folding into cone)* Circular paper sheets, Scissors, Protractor, Glue
V. CONCLUSION & SUMMARIZING (3 min) Concludes that a cone is 1/3rd of a cylinder in volume, and its CSA is half the product of base circumference and slant height. Write the summary and observe the relationship between the art activity and the formula. Summary:
Vol of Cone = (1/3) × Vol of Cylinder
CSA = πrl		 -
VI. CULMINATORY ACTIVITIES - EVALUATION (2 min) Asks 4 oral questions. Answer the questions orally. Evaluation:
Q1: Find l if r=3cm, h=4cm.
Ans: l = √(9+16) = 5 cm
Q2: Find CSA if r=7cm, l=10cm. [π=22/7]
Ans: (22/7)×7×10 = 220 cm²
Q3: What is the formula for TSA of a cone?
Ans: πr(l + r)
Q4: Volume of a cone is what fraction of a cylinder's volume?
Ans: 1/3		 -
Home Task (1 min) Assigns homework. Note down the homework. Home Task:
1. Find the CSA and TSA of a cone with radius 5.6 cm and slant height 9 cm. (Take π=22/7)
2. Find the volume of a cone with radius 3.5 cm and height 12 cm.		 -

PERIOD 5: Sphere and Hemisphere - Surface Area & Volume

Teaching Steps Teacher Activity Learner Activity Black Board Work TLM
I. INTRODUCTION (3-5 min)
*Mind Mapping* (2-3 min)
*Probing Questions* (2-3 min)
*Declaration of Topic* (2 min)		 Maps 'Spherical objects' (balls, globes). Asks: "What is half of a sphere called?" and "How many faces does a sphere have?" Declares the topic. List spherical objects. Answer: Hemisphere. One curved face. Note down the topic. Topic: Sphere and Hemisphere
Objects: Balls, Laddoos, Earth
Sphere ÷ 2 = Hemisphere		 Blackboard, Chalk
II. READING THE TEXTBOOK (5 min)
*Identifying Keywords* (2-3 min)		 Directs students to read page 234-238. Asks them to underline keywords related to the surface and volume of spheres. Read the text and underline keywords: Centre, Radius, Sphere, Hemisphere, Curved Surface Area. Keywords:
- Sphere
- Hemisphere
- Centre
- Solid		 Textbook
III. GROUP DISCUSSION (5 min)
*Concept Understanding* (5-7 min)		 Explains the tennis ball and string activity (4 circles = 1 sphere). Discusses formulas: TSA(Sphere) = 4πr², Vol = 4/3 πr³, and derives hemisphere formulas. Discuss how the surface area of a sphere equals the area of 4 circles of the same radius. Derive hemisphere formulas by halving the sphere. Formulas:
Sphere:
SA = 4πr²
Vol = (4/3)πr³
Hemisphere:
CSA = 2πr²
TSA = 3πr²
Vol = (2/3)πr³		 Tennis ball, String, 4 paper circles
IV. ART INTEGRATED ACTIVITY (10 min) Distributes chart paper and paints. Asks students to draw and paint a hemisphere poster, clearly labelling and colour-coding the flat circular base and the curved surface. Draw a large hemisphere on chart paper. Paint the curved surface in one colour and the flat circular base in another. Label them with their respective area formulas. *(Draw a labelled hemisphere showing curved area and flat base)* Chart paper, Paints/Colours, Markers
V. CONCLUSION & SUMMARIZING (3 min) Concludes that for a hemisphere, TSA includes the flat base, whereas CSA does not. Summarize the difference between CSA and TSA of a hemisphere. Summary:
For Hemisphere:
TSA = CSA + Area of base
3πr² = 2πr² + πr²		 -
VI. CULMINATORY ACTIVITIES - EVALUATION (2 min) Poses oral evaluation questions. Answer the questions orally. Evaluation:
Q1: Find SA of sphere r=3.5cm. [π=22/7]
Ans: 4×(22/7)×3.5×3.5 = 154 cm²
Q2: Find TSA of hemisphere r=7cm.
Ans: 3×(22/7)×7×7 = 462 cm²
Q3: What is the volume of a sphere?
Ans: (4/3)πr³
Q4: How many circles of radius 'r' make up the surface of a sphere?
Ans: 4		 -
Home Task (1 min) Assigns homework. Note down the homework. Home Task:
1. Find the volume of a sphere whose radius is 6.3 cm. (Take π=22/7)
2. Find the CSA and TSA of a hemisphere of radius 10 cm. (Take π=3.14)
3. Draw a neat diagram of a sphere showing centre and radius.		 -

Section B: Academic Standards

Sub-Standard Indicators
1. Conceptual Understanding
• 1.1 Identifies 2D/3D
• 1.2 Properties of 3D
• 1.3 Surface area concept
• 1.4 Volume concept		 • Differentiates between 2D figures (length, breadth) and 3D objects (length, breadth, height) and identifies their respective nets.
• Identifies and counts faces, edges, vertices, and understands properties like radius, height, and slant height in 3D solids.
• Understands that total surface area is the sum of areas of all faces, and lateral surface area excludes the top/base.
• Comprehends volume as the space occupied by a solid, measured in cubic units, and capacity as the volume of a hollow object.
2. Application of Concepts
• 2.1 Applies formulas
• 2.2 Volume in daily life
• 2.3 Effect of scaling		 • Substitutes accurate values (taking π = 22/7 or 3.14 as required) into derived formulas for TSA, LSA, and Volume of cuboids, cubes, cylinders, cones, and spheres.
• Solves real-world problems such as calculating the capacity of a tank, the cost of painting a cylindrical pillar, or the canvas required for a tent.
• Analyzes and computes the change in surface area and volume when the dimensions of a solid are doubled, tripled, or scaled by a factor of 'n'.
3. Problem Solving
• 3.1 Mathematical problems
• 3.2 Real-life problems
• 3.3 Diagrams		 • Solves textbook exercises and numerical problems involving unknown dimensions (e.g., finding radius when volume and height are given).
• Deconstructs complex, multi-step word problems (e.g., melting a cuboid to cast a cylinder) to find appropriate solutions.
• Draws accurate 2D representations (nets) and cross-sectional diagrams (right triangles in cones) to visually aid problem-solving.
4. Reasoning and Proof
• 4.1 Derives formulas
• 4.2 Establishes relationships		 • Logically derives the formula for the curved surface area of a cylinder and cone by visualizing their corresponding rectangular and sector nets.
• Establishes the mathematical relationship between the volume of a cone and a cylinder (Vol of Cone = 1/3 Vol of Cylinder) and a pyramid and prism through conceptual reasoning.
5. Communication
• 5.1 Mathematical terminology
• 5.2 Multiple ways of expression		 • Uses precise mathematical terms like 'lateral surface', 'slant height', 'right circular', 'hemisphere', and 'circumference' appropriately in written and oral responses.
• Expresses understanding through multiple mediums: writing formulas, drawing geometric nets, creating 3D art-integrated models, and explaining derivations during group discussions.
